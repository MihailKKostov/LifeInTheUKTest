# 1
Q: Which TWO are fundamental principles of British life?
+ Democracy
+ The rule of law
- Loyalty to your employer
- A duty to vote in every election
E: The five principles are **democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of different faiths and beliefs, and participation in community life**.

Q: Which of these is a fundamental principle of British life?
+ Individual liberty
- National service
- Free healthcare for visitors
- Compulsory religious worship
E: **Individual liberty** is one of the five fundamental principles.

Q: Which TWO are fundamental principles of British life?
+ Tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs
+ Participation in community life
- Extremism
- Intolerance of other religions
E: Tolerance and **participation in community life** are both principles. There is **no place for extremism or intolerance**.

Q: What are TWO responsibilities you will have as a UK resident or citizen?
+ To look after the area in which you live and the environment
+ To look after yourself and your family
- To stay in the UK forever
- To join a political party
E: You should **respect and obey the law, respect others' rights, treat others fairly, look after yourself and your family, and look after your area and the environment**.

Q: Which TWO freedoms does the UK offer its residents?
+ Freedom of speech
+ Freedom of belief and religion
- Freedom to ignore the law
- Freedom from paying tax
E: The UK offers **freedom of belief and religion, freedom of speech, freedom from unfair discrimination, a fair trial and a right to join in the election of a government**.

Q: Which TWO are rights offered by the UK to residents?
+ A right to a fair trial
+ Freedom from unfair discrimination
- A right to free housing
- A right to vote at the age of 16
E: The UK offers **a right to a fair trial** and **freedom from unfair discrimination**, among others.

Q: British values are based on which TWO things?
+ History
+ Traditions
- Wealth
- Religion only
E: British values are **based on history and traditions** and are protected by law, customs and expectations.

Q: There is a place in British society for extremism and intolerance.
- True
+ False
E: There is **no place in British society for extremism or intolerance**.

Q: Which statement is correct?
+ New citizens pledge to uphold British values at their citizenship ceremony
- New citizens pledge to join the armed forces at their citizenship ceremony
E: At the **citizenship ceremony** new citizens make a **pledge** to uphold British values and observe the law.

Q: How many questions are in the Life in the UK test?
+ 24
- 20
- 30
- 40
E: The test has **24 questions** about important aspects of life in the UK.

Q: What percentage do you need to pass the Life in the UK test?
+ 75%
- 50%
- 60%
- 90%
E: You need **75% – 18 out of 24 correct**.

Q: Life in the UK test questions are based only on the history chapter of the handbook.
- True
+ False
E: Questions are based on **all parts of the handbook**, including the introduction.

Q: Which TWO languages, besides English, can the Life in the UK test be taken in by special arrangement?
+ Welsh
+ Scottish Gaelic
- French
- Irish Gaelic
E: The test can be taken in **Welsh or Scottish Gaelic** by special arrangement.

Q: How can you book the Life in the UK test?
+ Online only
- By telephone
- At a post office
- At any public library
E: You can **only book online**, and must take the test at a registered centre.

Q: What must you take with you to the Life in the UK test?
+ Identification and proof of your address
- Your birth certificate and a passport photo
- A letter from your employer
- Your national insurance card only
E: You need **identification and proof of your address** or you will not be able to take the test.

Q: About how many Life in the UK test centres are there?
+ 60
- 10
- 200
- 500
E: There are **about 60** registered and approved test centres around the UK.

Q: You can take the Life in the UK test at any college or school.
- True
+ False
E: You must take the test at a **registered and approved test centre** – other certificates are not accepted.

Q: Which TWO places have different arrangements for taking the Life in the UK test?
+ The Isle of Man
+ The Channel Islands
- Northern Ireland
- Wales
E: People living on the **Isle of Man** or in the **Channel Islands** have different arrangements.

Q: Which statement is correct?
+ To settle in the UK you need to pass the Life in the UK test and show English at B1 level
- To settle in the UK you only need to have lived here for one year
E: For settlement you must **pass the Life in the UK test** and show speaking and listening skills in English at **B1** (ESOL Entry Level 3).
