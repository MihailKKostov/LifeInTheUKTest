@title The values and principles of the UK
@subtitle British values, your rights and responsibilities, and the test
@intro This short chapter is fully testable. Expect questions on the **five fundamental principles**, the **responsibilities and freedoms** of residents, and how the Life in the UK test works.

@section British values
British society is built on shared values that everyone living in the UK is expected to respect. They come from **history and tradition** and are protected by **law, customs and expectations**. There is **no place in British society for extremism or intolerance**.

## The fundamental principles of British life
- **Democracy**
- **The rule of law**
- **Individual liberty**
- **Tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs**
- **Participation in community life**

! A common trick is to offer a "principle" that sounds plausible but is not on this list, such as "a duty to vote" or "loyalty to your employer". Learn the five exactly.

## The citizenship pledge
New citizens promise to uphold these values at their citizenship ceremony:

" I will give my loyalty to the United Kingdom and respect its rights and freedoms.
" I will uphold its democratic values.
" I will observe its laws faithfully and fulfil my duties and obligations as a British citizen.
"— The citizenship pledge

## What is expected of you
If you want to be a permanent resident or citizen of the UK, you should:
- **respect and obey the law**
- **respect the rights of others**, including their right to their own opinions
- **treat others with fairness**
- **look after yourself and your family**
- **look after the area in which you live and the environment**

## What the UK offers in return
- **freedom of belief and religion**
- **freedom of speech**
- **freedom from unfair discrimination**
- **a right to a fair trial**
- **a right to join in the election of a government**

! Questions often ask you to pick "two responsibilities" or "two freedoms". Responsibilities are things you do (obey the law, look after your family); freedoms are things you receive (free speech, fair trial).

@remember
- The five principles: **democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of other faiths and beliefs, participation in community life**
- British values are based on **history and traditions** and protected by **law, customs and expectations**
- There is **no place for extremism or intolerance**
- New citizens make a **pledge** of loyalty at a **citizenship ceremony**
- Responsibilities: **obey the law, respect others' rights, treat others fairly, look after yourself and your family, look after your area and the environment**
- Freedoms: **belief and religion, speech, freedom from unfair discrimination, a fair trial, joining in the election of a government**

@section Becoming a permanent resident
To become a permanent resident or citizen you must **speak and read English** and have **a good understanding of life in the UK**.

For settlement you need to **pass the Life in the UK test** and show **speaking and listening skills in English at B1** of the Common European Framework of Reference (equivalent to **ESOL Entry Level 3**).

The handbook is written so that anyone who reads English at **ESOL Entry Level 3** can understand it. People below that level can take an **ESOL course in English with Citizenship** instead, which ends with its own test.

@remember
- You must **speak and read English** and understand **life in the UK**
- Settlement needs the **Life in the UK test** plus English at **B1** (= **ESOL Entry Level 3**)
- Below that level you can take an **ESOL course in English with Citizenship**

@section Taking the Life in the UK test
## The format
- **24 questions** about important aspects of life in the UK
- Questions are based on **all parts of the handbook**, including the introduction
- Every candidate at a session gets **different questions**
- You have **45 minutes** and need **75% (18 correct)** to pass

## Booking and sitting the test
- The test is usually taken in **English**, but can be taken in **Welsh or Scottish Gaelic** by special arrangement.
- It can only be taken at a **registered and approved test centre** – there are about **60** around the UK.
- You can **only book online**.
- You must bring **identification and proof of address**, or you will not be allowed to sit the test.
- The **Isle of Man and the Channel Islands** have different arrangements.

! Only certificates from registered test centres are accepted – a test taken anywhere else does not count.

@remember
- **24 questions** from **the whole handbook**, **45 minutes**, pass mark **75% (18/24)**
- About **60 registered test centres**; booking is **online only**
- Can be taken in **Welsh or Scottish Gaelic** by special arrangement
- Bring **ID and proof of address**
- **Isle of Man and Channel Islands** have different arrangements
